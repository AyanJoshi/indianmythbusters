About Indian Myth Busters

We exist for one reason: to replace misinformation with evidence, so people can make better decisions for themselves and their families.

Why This Exists

India is a country of incredible knowledge, ancient wisdom, and brilliant minds. But mixed in with genuine wisdom are countless myths and misconceptions that get passed down through generations, amplified by WhatsApp forwards, and rarely questioned.

Some of these myths are harmless. Others are dangerous -- leading people to avoid proper medical treatment, follow ineffective remedies, or make decisions based on fear instead of facts.

Indian Myth Busters is a simple project with a clear goal: take common beliefs, investigate them using legitimate scientific sources, and present the evidence clearly so you can decide for yourself.

Our Principles

📚

Evidence First

Every verdict is based on peer-reviewed research, government health bodies (WHO, NIH, ICMR), or established scientific institutions. No personal opinions.

🔗

Sources Always Cited

Every myth investigation includes direct links to the sources used. We want you to verify our claims, not just take our word for it.

⚖

Nuance Matters

Not everything is black and white. When a myth is partially true, we say so, and explain exactly what part holds up and what does not.

👍

Respectful Approach

We are not here to mock anyone for believing myths. These beliefs are often deeply rooted and culturally significant. We simply present the science.

What Sources Do We Use?

We prioritize the following types of sources, in order of reliability:

  • Peer-reviewed journal articles (PubMed, The Lancet, BMJ, JAMA, etc.)
  • Government health organizations (WHO, NIH, ICMR, NIN, CDC)
  • Established medical institutions (Mayo Clinic, Harvard Health, Cleveland Clinic)
  • Reputable fact-checking organizations (Snopes, when backed by primary sources)

We avoid anecdotal evidence, self-published blogs, and sources without clear methodology.

Want to Contribute?

The best way to help is to submit myths you have heard so we can investigate them. If you are a researcher, doctor, or scientist and want to help verify or correct our content, we welcome that collaboration.

This is an open project. If any of our investigations contain errors, we want to know. Point us to the evidence and we will update our verdict.
